NR quality per carrier: the NR samples of each group per NR band, with their share of the group, median SS-RSRP and SS-SINR and the share in each quality class, so a carrier that reaches a lower quality than the others stands out.

With LTE+NR, LTE and NR are analysed separately, each with its own thresholds. Curves keep the Operator/Vendor colour; the latest campaign is drawn thickest and older campaigns progressively thinner. Quality classes — RSRP: Excellent ≥ -80, Good ≥ -90, Fair ≥ -100, Poor ≥ -110 dBm, Very poor below. SINR: Excellent ≥ 20, Good ≥ 13, Fair ≥ 5, Poor ≥ 0 dB, Bad below. Voice uses the measuring (A) side.

Failed: their result is not completed; the round trip is the median of each passed test (Interactivity_RTT_Median), shown as the median and the slowest tenth (P90) of the tests; packet loss: the share of passed tests that lost a packet; jitter: the median delay variation of the tests. The shade ranks the groups of each environment, from the best (green) to the worst (red).

Download tests by location

No analysis yet.

Bandwidth per band: the samples of each serving band per DL bandwidth of its primary carrier (LTE PCell or NR PCell). Bandwidth of each test (LTE + NR): each test's LTE bandwidth (its mean over the aggregated LTE carriers, else its LTE PCell bandwidth, rounded up to 5 MHz) plus its NR PCell bandwidth; the CDRs do not report the bandwidth of NR secondary carriers, so NR carrier aggregation is not added. Each block is as wide as the midpoint of its class and as tall as its share of the tests with a known bandwidth; the figure beside it is the share below 25 MHz and the one under it the share at 100 MHz or more. Ladders per kind of serving site or RAN Vendor split each group by the cell that served the start of the tests (the serving-cell Vendor Mapping), keeping the combinations with 20 tests or more of a known bandwidth. Download tests by location: per City (the most measured ones, with 15 tests or more in a group), the share of the download tests under 25 MHz and the share that failed or reached 5 Mbit/s or less. Big cities (driven and walked) are Cities, medium and small cities are Towns, and connecting roads, highways and train routes are Roads and railways.
