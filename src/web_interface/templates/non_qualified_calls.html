                      Status Rules

                      Change these rules with care. Modifying them without the necessary knowledge of the fields and statuses of the calls can completely change how the calls are classified: on Save, every call with an automatic status takes the status the new rules give it.

                        DT Analyzer RCA Rules

                        How the DT Analyzer RCA finds the Root Category and Root Cause of every Non-Qualified Call from the data of its CDR (its failure, the LTE and NR radio of both sides, its mobility and its KPIs), in every workspace whatever its CDR type. The rules are tried from top to bottom: the first enabled rule of the service of the call whose conditions hold (all of them, or any of them) gives its Root Category and Root Cause on the Root Cause Catalog, and their domain. A rule without conditions takes every call of its services that reaches it; a text condition holds when the text contains one of its words (separated by commas; "a+b" needs both). Saving calculates every call again.

                        {% if not nq_can_configure %}{{ role_warning('the DT Analyzer RCA Rules') }}{% endif %}

                        Change these rules with care. Their order matters: a rule takes the calls the rules above it leave, so a broad rule high in the list hides the rules below it. On Save, every call takes the root cause the new rules give it.

                        A call takes the Root Category and Root Cause the analysts gave most of the other calls of its pattern (the same rule, service and failure of the provider), when at least 2 of them and more than half agree; the rules give the others. It also learns for the calls the rules leave Not identified.

                          Prioritization Rules

                          Which Non-Qualified Calls are analysed first, and by which team. Saving applies the strategy to every call at once; the Priority of an open call is filled while nobody chose another one by hand, and never changes once its analysis has started.

                          {% if not nq_can_prioritize %}{{ role_warning('the Prioritization Rules') }}{% endif %}
                          {# The strategy and its actions in one line across the dialog; the steps below on the left; the calls per queue and the levels on the right, at the height of the steps and in sight while they scroll. #}
                          How the strategies work

                          Strategies

                          • A strategy gives every call a step, which sets its Priority (P1 to P8) and its queue.
                          • Dual Queue (Cities first / Roads first): during the campaign by what the call says about itself; once the Final CDR is scored by its real impact. They differ only in the place of SA Voice (Cities or Connecting Roads first).
                          • Dual Queue (Scoring Based): every call by its impact level (list 2 of every strategy), list 1 alone.
                          • Vodafone Proposal: SA before NSA and Voice before Data; every Voice and WhatsApp call, the Data calls of a cluster, then every other call (P5); one Unique queue, list 1 alone.
                          • New Strategy copies the one shown; Rename Strategy renames any; Delete Strategy deletes your own. Save applies the strategy shown.

                          Lists and queues

                          • List 1 (Weekly CDRs): while the Final CDR of the campaign is not scored.
                          • List 2 (Final CDRs): once it is scored, if the strategy uses it (Use List 2 for Final CDRs); else list 1 goes on.
                          • Voice queue: Voice and WhatsApp calls. Data queue: Data tests. Unique queue: every call of a strategy of one queue. Tracked: in no queue.

                          Steps

                          • A call takes the first step it meets, by Priority and then by row (the upper row first).
                          • Conditions join with AND / OR (AND first); AND ( and OR ( group them.
                          • An SA call of an operator no SA step takes goes to its NSA steps.
                          • Operators by default: the Focus operators (SA steps: those that use SA).

                          Queue order

                          1. Priority
                          2. Newest campaign
                          3. Step (upper row first)
                          4. Highest Expected Gain
                          5. Oldest call

                          Impact levels

                          • Points lost by a call: in each KPI it misses, the points its operator loses in the environment, shared among the tests that miss it.
                          • Expected Gain: during the campaign, what the failure lost in the Final CDR of the latest campaign of its NR Mode; once its own Final CDR is scored, its own points.
                          • The calls in scope of a campaign, operator and service (those out of scope never count), sorted by Expected Gain (most first), add up their share of it:
                          • High impact: the calls within the first %.
                          • Medium impact: the next ones, up to %. Low impact: the rest, or no Expected Gain.
                          • The Scoring Impact column of the calls keeps the points of the CDRs scored so far.

                          Other conditions

                          • Cluster: at least calls of the operator within m of the call (or at its site) in its campaign and the campaigns before it (the Ping tests and the calls out of scope apart).
                          • Consecutive failures: another failure of the operator in the same drive (the car) within min, whatever its call type and wherever it was.
                          • Hotspots: 2+ failures of an operator, NR Mode and campaign, whatever their call type, each within min (with OR, in the same drive) m (or at the site where it ended) of another one. Drops and Setup failures: by the result of the call.

                          Out of scope and shared settings

                          • Out of Scope: its regions, the operators that are not Focus operators and the NR Mode · Operator pairs chosen (SA · Three): no queue, not tracked.
                          • The operators excluded in the Operator Maps count nowhere.
                          • Shared by every strategy: Out of Scope, the levels on the right and Fill the Priority; they apply at once.
                          • Fill the Priority: an open call takes the Priority of its step until someone changes it, and keeps it once its analysis starts.
