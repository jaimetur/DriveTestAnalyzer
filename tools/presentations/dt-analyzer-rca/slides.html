Inputs · Layer3 Logs

What the log of the call adds

When the campaign of a call has Layer3 Logs, its session (found by its JOIN_ID; both UEs of a Voice call) tells the rules what the UE measured and signalled right before the failure.

Radio

Radio at the failure

RSRP, RSRQ and SINR of the LTE and NR PCell in its last 5 seconds.

RRC

Failures of the RRC

SCG failures, RRC re-establishments and releases, and the rejects of the network.

Mobility

Handovers

Handovers and inter-RAT changes, and a stronger neighbour on the serving channel with no handover to it.

IMS

SIP

The worst SIP response of the session (401, 407, 487 and 489, the normal ones, left out).

Radio

Uplink and BLER

The UE at its maximum power (22 dBm or more) and the BLER of LTE and NR.

Device

No measurements

A session that never measured the radio: the device or the test setup.

12 rules read the log first, before the radio of the CDR; the rules of rejects, authentication, TAU, IMS, handover, radio link and inter-RAT read it too. Its evidence quotes the log, and a log imported or deleted calculates again the calls of its campaign.

Lack of resources

Three ways to see that the network ran short

From what the provider reports, from the signalling of the CDR itself and from the resource blocks a test was given.

The core rejects

The provider reports insufficient resources or congestion: a PDU Session Establishment Reject with the 5GSM cause Insufficient resources, a CM Service Reject for congestion.

CoreCore NetworkInsufficient resources

The access is rejected

The signalling of the CDR itself, without any RCA of the provider: RRCConnectionReject, RRCReject, Immediate Assignment Reject.

RANRAN CapacityAccess rejected

Few resource blocks

A Data test cut off by time with good radio whose carrier gave it less than 25 % of its resource blocks: the cell was loaded. A completed test usually gets about two thirds.

RANRAN CapacityCell overload
resource blocks used: < 25 % loaded cell · ≈ 66 % a completed test

The network around the call

Far is relative: the inter-site distance

A city site serves a few hundred metres, a rural or road site several kilometres. Every distance is compared with the ISD of the site: the mean distance to its 3 nearest sites of the same network, from the Network Inventory.

City

ISD ≈ 1 km

Overshooting: served from 3 km with sites every kilometre.

Road and country

ISD ≈ 3–6 km

Expected: the same 3 km where the sites are far apart.

No neighbour lists nor Timing Advance needed: the positions of the sites are enough. A site with no other site of its network within 40 km has no ISD.

The network around the call

What the Network Inventory reveals

The same criteria as the Failure Map of the call, checked before the plain coverage and interference they bring.

Coverage hole

The nearest site of the networks of the operator is at least 1.5 × its ISD away, with RSRP below −110 dBm or no radio at all.

RFPoor Coverage LTE / NRLarge distance to the nearest site

Overshooting

The serving site is at least 2 × its ISD and 1 km away, with SINR below 3 dB or RSRP below −105 dBm; the nearest site hints at a missing neighbour.

PCI collision
